## Design notes

- **Hero:** The hard-route headline, summit challenge, durability details, and small climber turn the pack into equipment for a self-chosen test of effort. The language celebrates resolve without inventing an enemy or shaming rest.
- **Postmodern:** The graphic quotes familiar trail signage and mountain imagery, then recombines them with an oversized pack emblem, deliberately offset labels, clashing scenic planes, asymmetry, and layered frames. Its references stay legible while the composition is theatrical and playfully unresolved, drawing on the project’s Vanna Venturi and Bilbao examples.
- **Consistency:** The “next-mile standard” makes a small, voluntary commitment visible before the CTA. The visitor chooses a promise connected to their own trip, then can select gear that supports it. The prompt explicitly allows changing the choice; it uses follow-through as encouragement, not a trap.
- **Audience and offer:** Reinforced construction and field repair speak to people who value dependable heavy-duty gear; the direct, action-led copy suits adventurous customers.
