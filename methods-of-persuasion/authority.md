# Authority

## What It Means

Authority is the idea that people may be more likely to trust information or follow guidance from someone they see as knowledgeable, experienced, or qualified.

## How It Works

Authority can influence decisions because people often rely on experts when they are unsure what to do. Credentials, relevant experience, and clear explanations can help an audience judge whether a recommendation is trustworthy.

Authority should be relevant to the topic. A person’s title or popularity does not automatically make them an expert, and claims should not exaggerate their qualifications.

## Examples

- A dentist explains how to care for teeth using current health guidance.
- A qualified financial adviser describes the risks of an investment.
- A product page shares test results from an independent, qualified reviewer.

## Design and Branding

A brand can communicate authority by clearly presenting relevant credentials, sources, and evidence. Design can make these details easy to find, while keeping claims specific and understandable. Professional appearance can support a message, but should not substitute for accurate information.

## Example Phrasing

- “Reviewed by a licensed nutritionist.”
- “Our recommendations are based on published research.”
- “This guide was prepared by experienced instructors.”
- “See the independent test results.”

## Important Note

Authority should be demonstrated honestly. Do not invent credentials, imply endorsements that do not exist, or present unsupported claims as expert advice.
