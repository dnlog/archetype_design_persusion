# Reciprocity

## What It Means

Reciprocity is the idea that people may feel inclined to respond positively after receiving something helpful or valuable.

## How It Works

Reciprocity can influence decisions when someone receives a useful resource, service, or favor and wants to return the gesture. A free sample or helpful guide may build goodwill, but it should not create an obligation to buy or participate.

The value and any conditions should be clear. People should know whether an offer is free and whether accepting it involves sharing information or agreeing to anything.

## Examples

- A library offers a free guide to using its research resources.
- A store provides a sample without requiring a purchase.
- A community organization shares useful event-planning materials with the public.

## Design and Branding

A brand can offer genuine value through useful resources, samples, or assistance. Make the terms easy to understand and avoid placing unexpected requirements on the offer. Any follow-up marketing should respect people’s communication preferences.

## Example Phrasing

- “Download this guide at no cost.”
- “Try a sample before deciding whether to buy.”
- “This resource is free and does not require a purchase.”
- “You can opt out of future emails at any time.”

## Important Note

Reciprocity should not be used to make people feel indebted. Avoid hidden conditions, unwanted follow-ups, or suggesting that a gift requires a purchase.
