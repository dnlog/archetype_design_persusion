# Scarcity

## What It Means

Scarcity is the idea that people may value something more when it is limited. The limit might be time, quantity, access, or availability. When an item could disappear soon, people often pay closer attention to it.

## How It Works

Scarcity can influence decisions because people do not want to miss an opportunity. A limited number of tickets, a short registration period, or a product that is nearly sold out can make someone decide sooner than they otherwise would.

This method should communicate a real limit, not create a fake emergency. “Only two seats left” is useful information if only two seats are actually left. If the number is invented, the message becomes misleading.

## Examples

- A concert website shows that only a small number of tickets remain for a real event.
- A clothing website notes that a particular size is currently almost sold out.
- A student club announces a real deadline for signing up for a limited-capacity trip.

## Design and Branding

A brand can make scarcity easy to notice with a clear deadline, a small inventory message, or a countdown that shows a real ending time. A contrasting color can draw attention to the limited item, while the rest of the page remains easy to read. The layout should still show important details such as price, return policies, and product information instead of hiding them behind urgency.

## Example Phrasing

- “Registration closes Friday at 5:00 p.m.”
- “A few seats remain for this session.”
- “This color is currently low in stock.”
- “Available while this limited batch lasts.”

## Important Note

Scarcity should be used honestly. A brand should not invent low stock, fake deadlines, or misleading countdowns just to pressure people into buying.
