# Social Proof

## What It Means

Social proof is the idea that people often look at what other people are doing when they are unsure what to choose. Reviews, recommendations, attendance numbers, and examples of real customers can all act as social proof.

## How It Works

When a decision feels unfamiliar, people may use other people’s experiences as helpful evidence. For example, a student choosing a new study app may feel more confident after reading detailed reviews from other students. Social proof can reduce uncertainty, but it should not replace a person’s own judgment.

The quality of the information matters. A detailed, honest review is more useful than a vague claim that “everyone loves it.”

## Examples

- A restaurant website displays real customer reviews and comments about menu items.
- An online store includes verified buyer feedback about fit, size, or delivery.
- A campus event page shows photos from past events and quotes from actual attendees.

## Design and Branding

A brand can use social proof through readable reviews, customer photos used with permission, ratings, case studies, or examples of people using the product. A design should clearly label reviews and avoid making them look like independent opinions if they were sponsored. A simple layout with names or relevant details can make feedback easier to evaluate.

## Example Phrasing

- “Read feedback from verified customers.”
- “See how students used this service.”
- “Recommended by attendees from last year’s event.”
- “Customer reviews include fit and sizing details.”

## Important Note

Social proof should be real and relevant. Brands should not invent reviews, hide that an endorsement was paid for, or imply that a small group represents everyone.
