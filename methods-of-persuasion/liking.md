# Liking

## What It Means

Liking is the idea that people may be more open to a message from someone they find relatable, appealing, or friendly.

## How It Works

Liking can influence decisions through shared interests, warm communication, or a sense of familiarity. When people feel understood, they may be more willing to pay attention to a recommendation.

A friendly tone should not be used to disguise important information or create a false sense of friendship. The message should remain honest and respectful.

## Examples

- A local bookstore recommends books based on readers’ stated interests.
- A community group uses welcoming language to invite new members.
- A company shares a staff member’s genuine experience with a product.

## Design and Branding

A brand can encourage connection through approachable language, relatable stories, and imagery that reflects its audience. The design should represent people respectfully and avoid relying on stereotypes. Important details should remain clear, even in friendly or casual messaging.

## Example Phrasing

- “Looking for your next weekend read?”
- “Here are a few options based on what you told us you enjoy.”
- “Our team is happy to help you compare choices.”
- “See how other local members use this service.”

## Important Note

Liking should come from genuine connection, not deception. Do not fake personal relationships, fabricate testimonials, or use flattery to pressure people.
