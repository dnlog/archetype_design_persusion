# Consistency

## What It Means

Consistency is the idea that people often want their actions to align with commitments, values, or decisions they have already made.

## How It Works

Consistency can influence decisions when someone makes a small commitment and later considers a related action. For example, choosing a personal goal may encourage someone to take steps that support it.

A commitment should be freely chosen and easy to understand. People should be able to change their minds without being shamed or trapped by an earlier decision.

## Examples

- A person who sets a goal to walk more signs up for a weekly walking group.
- A customer who chooses reusable packaging opts into it for future orders.
- A student who plans study time uses a calendar reminder to follow through.

## Design and Branding

A brand can support consistency with clear progress indicators, saved preferences, or reminders connected to goals a person selected. These features should make it easy to review or change a choice. The design should avoid turning a small action into pressure to make a larger commitment.

## Example Phrasing

- “You chose a weekly activity goal of three walks.”
- “Would you like to keep your reusable packaging preference?”
- “Your plan is saved. You can change it at any time.”
- “You’re making progress toward the goal you set.”

## Important Note

Consistency should support people’s own intentions, not exploit past choices. Avoid guilt, hidden commitments, or making cancellation difficult.
